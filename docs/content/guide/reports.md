# Reports and report cards

Three different things, for three different questions.

| | Answers | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reports** tab | "How is each student doing right now?" | Inside a class |
| **Class report** | "How is the class doing, and who is where?" | Inside a class |
| **Report card** | "What goes home to parents?" | Generated as a PDF |

## Live grades

The **Reports** tab inside a class shows grades calculated from the marks entered so far. Pick a term and you get every student's subject grades as they currently stand.

Nothing here is a snapshot. Mark something tonight and this changes tonight. That makes it the right place to check a term *before* committing report cards, and the wrong thing to quote as final.

If a student looks wrong, work back: is every piece of work marked? Is anything excluded that should not be? Does the grading scheme total 100?

## Class report

The **Class report** is the class as a whole: subject averages, highest and lowest, and student rankings.

Use it to spot a subject sitting well below the others, or a student whose position has moved sharply. Rankings are computed across the students in that class for that term.

## Report cards

Report cards are PDFs, generated per student per term.

From a class's **Reports** tab, **Download all (PDFs)** produces the whole class in one go — the usual end-of-term action. It takes a moment for a large class; the page tells you it is preparing. Individual students can be downloaded one at a time from the student report view, and a year-end card is produced the same way.

A report card carries the student's subject grades, their overall average and position, attendance for the term, and remarks.

### Before you generate

Because the numbers are calculated at the moment you press the button, get these right first — regenerating afterwards is easy, but sending the wrong one home is not:

1. Every piece of work for the term is marked.
2. Anything that should not count is excluded rather than zeroed.
3. The attendance register is complete for the term.
4. The grading scheme's weights total 100.
5. The grade scale is the one you intend.

### Draft, published, sent

A report card is **draft** while you are working on it, **published** once it is ready, and can be marked **sent to ministry** where that applies. Publishing is what makes it visible to the student in their portal — before that, they see nothing.

## What students see

Students see their own reports in the portal, and only after publishing. They never see class rankings against other students by name, nor anyone else's marks.

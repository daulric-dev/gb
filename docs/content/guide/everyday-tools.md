# Files, messages and announcements

The parts of GradeBook that are not about marks.

## Files

**Files** is your own storage plus anything shared with you — and it is where generated report cards land.

Upload by choosing a file. Large files on a weak connection resume rather than restarting, so a dropped connection is not a lost upload.

**What you can upload:** PDFs, Word documents, spreadsheets, plain text, CSV, and images (PNG, JPEG, WebP). Up to 10MB each.

Anything else is refused. So is a file whose contents do not match its name — renaming a program to `.pdf` does not get it in. Documents carrying macros and PDFs with embedded actions are refused too. If an upload is rejected and you believe the file is fine, re-save it from the application that made it; that usually clears whatever the original was carrying.

**Sharing** a file with a colleague notifies them, and it appears in their Files. You can stop sharing later. A file is reachable by its owner, the people it is shared with, and — for a student's submission — the teacher who owns the class. Nobody else, including other administrators.

## Messages

**Messages** is direct messaging with others at your school. Pick a person and type.

Messages are encrypted where they are stored, and the unread count sits next to Messages in the menu.

Group channels are not available yet.

## Announcements

**Announcements** are notices for staff across the school — a staff meeting, a deadline, a policy change.

Post one and it appears for staff, with an unread marker until read. Announcements do not go to students; for something a class needs to know, put it in the instructions on the work itself, or message them.

## Settings

Your name, profile picture and the school you belong to. Your email address is your identity and is not editable here — an administrator changes it if it has to change.

The light/dark toggle is in the top corner of every page and follows your device by default.

### Deleting your account

**Danger Zone** at the bottom of Settings deletes your account. Read this before using it:

- It removes **your login**, not the school's records. Work students handed in, marks you entered and reports you generated stay where they are — a school's records are not one person's to withdraw.
- It cannot be undone. Signing up again with the same address gives you a new, empty account that has to be re-approved and re-assigned to classes.
- If you are the **only administrator**, deal with that first. Give someone else administrator access before you go, or the school is left with nobody who can approve staff, manage the roster or issue join codes.

If you are simply leaving the school, ask an administrator to remove your access instead. That is the reversible version.

## Using the mobile app

The app covers the same ground as the web: staff take attendance, set and mark work, and read reports; students see their work, grades, attendance and reports.

A few differences worth knowing:

- **Pull down to refresh** on any screen that lists things.
- Signing in works exactly as on the web — email, then the 8-digit code.
- You stay signed in between launches. Sign out from the menu on a shared device.
- Large uploads resume after a dropped connection, same as the web.

If the app cannot reach the server it says so plainly — *"Can't reach the server. Check your connection."* That is a network problem rather than a sign-in problem, and it is worth checking your connection before trying another email address.
